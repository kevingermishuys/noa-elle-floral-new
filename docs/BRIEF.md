## 2. Her customers

Two clear groups, and the site has to hold both without going mushy.

**A. The estate gift-buyer — the volume, and the one who converts today.**
Mostly men on Sable Hills / Pebble Rock / Leeufontein. Anniversary, apology, birthday, Mother's Day, "make up for the 19th hole". On a phone, often same-day, mildly panicked, does not want to browse. Needs: what it costs, when it arrives, one button to order. Free delivery to his own estate is the hook.

**B. The wedding/event planner.** Bride or coordinator, planning months ahead, on Instagram at night comparing florists. Wants to see scale, taste, and range of palette. Judges on photographs, not on words.

**Deal-killers:** no price, no delivery info, and — for group B — a thin gallery.

**Device reality:** phone, mobile data, often inside an estate with mediocre signal. This is the single biggest constraint on the gallery.

## 10. Design plan

**Direction:** a dark, quiet studio room where the flowers are the only bright thing on the screen.

Every florist in her area is pale cream with a serif. Her photographs are overwhelmingly white and ivory blooms — which disappear on a cream page and *blaze* on a dark one. So the site inverts the category: deep bark and olive as the dominant field, warm stone reserved for the reading sections. It's earthy as asked, but earthy the way the timber arch and eucalyptus are earthy, not the way a cream-and-terracotta template is.

**Palette**

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--bark` | `#241F1A` | dominant field, hero and gallery ground — the timber arch at dusk |
| `--olive` | `#4E5340` | secondary surface, section shifts — eucalyptus and olive foliage |
| `--stone` | `#E8E1D6` | light reading surface, body text on dark |
| `--pampas` | `#C7B49A` | rules, captions, quiet detail — dried pampas and kraft |
| `--protea` | `#B87B70` | the one accent: buttons, links, hover — king protea bract |

Five colours, all pulled from things physically present in her work. The accent is a dusty protea pink, not terracotta — deliberately, because cream-plus-terracotta is the exact combination we don't ship.

**Type:** Gambetta (display) + General Sans (body), both Fontshare. Gambetta has the calligraphic stress that rhymes with the hand-drawn lilies in her logo without being another Playfair. General Sans keeps the menu and delivery info plain and legible at 16px on a phone.

**Layout concept:** hero is one full-bleed dusk photograph, dark, with a short teasing line and two buttons — *WhatsApp an order* and *See the flowers*. Below it the delivery estates named in plain text, because for group A that free-delivery line is the whole pitch. Gallery is a masonry grid on bark, images doing all the talking.

**Signature element:** gallery tiles name their stems. Tap or hover a photo and it names what's actually in it — *king protea, lisianthus, veronica, bleached ruscus*. It turns the gallery from decoration into evidence of craft, it's honest to "uniquely composed using the finest seasonal blooms", and no competitor does it.

**Motion:** one moment only — a slow image reveal as the gallery loads. Nothing else moves.

### Speed plan (Kevin's "not slow")

A gallery is the natural enemy of a fast site, so this is decided up front, not patched later:

- Every image converted to WebP, three sizes, served with `srcset` — phones never download the desktop file
- Hard budget: **under 400 KB for the first screen**, under 1.2 MB for a full gallery scroll
- `loading="lazy"` on everything below the fold, `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero image only
- Blur-up placeholders so the layout never jumps on a slow connection
- Two font files, subset to the weights used
- No slider library, no lightbox library, no jQuery. The whole thing is HTML, CSS and roughly 40 lines of JavaScript.

### Self-check

- Would this design suit any other florist? No — the inversion is driven by her specific white-on-dark photographs and her protea work.
- Banned tells: no cream-and-terracotta, no 01/02/03 markers, no glassmorphism, no emoji icons, no scattered scroll animation. Clear.
